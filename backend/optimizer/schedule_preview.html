Notes from this round

Reverse-engineered from a real, human-tuned week. Gym, breakfast, lunch, and dinner now sit on the calendar as real fixed blocks (not tasks) — the same mechanism as a locked class. Friday and Saturday night are hard-protected (no work 7pm–midnight), Sunday gets its own much lower load cap instead of the same number as every other day, and a new preference rewards studying a course shortly after that course's own lecture — the "notes right after class" pattern real students actually use.

Urgency-aware prioritization, new this round: a task's sessions now get pulled earlier in proportion to real urgency (remaining work per hour of runway), not treated as interchangeable with everything else in scope. HW3 (10.75 hours, due in under a week) now finishes a day-plus ahead of its deadline instead of getting mixed in with far-off, lower-stakes reading — exactly the "why is it working on next week's stuff instead of the huge urgent thing" complaint this fixes. 1hr/subject is still a hard cap (decompose.py's session-length limit); it turned out to need no new mechanism.

The 2-hour rule is now a soft goal, not a hard wall — minimize the single longest unbroken stretch (chained by gaps under 45 min), so a genuinely urgent push isn't forced to drop a session just to satisfy an absolute cutoff. Getting the weight right took real tuning: 20–120 barely moved the worst streak at all (urgency and the after-class bonus simply won every tradeoff against it), 500–1000 brought a 210-minute worst case down to 90–150 with zero cost to sessions placed or to urgency's own job, and 3000 started measurably eating into that — HW3's last session got pushed to right before its own deadline. Landed on 1000.

Titles now lead with the class number ("21-241 Study for Recitation 3"), matching how a real calendar reads at a glance, and multi-part tasks share one repeated title instead of a "(part i/N)" suffix. A dedicated "Review Notes" session is also generated after every lecture, not just when a real assignment happens to be due.

Two real bugs found while verifying the break rule, unrelated to the rule itself: an unplaced session's status silently defaulted back to "flexible" instead of "unplaced," so a session that lost the scheduling squeeze just vanished from every report instead of showing up as "competed and lost." Separately, a review session's earliest-start bound floored a lecture's end time to the nearest 15-minute slot, letting it start up to 14 minutes before the lecture it was reviewing actually ended. Both only became visible once a harder model started producing real squeezes and real edge cases to hide.

Compute time, and a real caveat on it: nine preferences deep, this model shows real run-to-run variance at 10–15 seconds — the same weights produced a 210-minute worst streak at one budget and a 90-minute one at another, a few seconds apart. Not fully converged (CP-SAT's own gap sits around 0.1–0.3%, not the near-zero this prototype saw with fewer preferences). Bumped the default to 15s; still worth more headroom before trusting a single run's exact numbers.

Still-open gap: Midterm 1 / Exam 1 are still movable blocks with a deadline, not a fixed time like a lecture — a schema question (see backend/optimizer/README.md), not a preference.
